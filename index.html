PHILOSOPHICAL ARGUMENT LABORATORY · V4

Build, test, and challenge an argument

Construct a claim from premises, map objections and responses, and distinguish formal validity from the truth of premises. This tool visualizes reasoning; it does not automatically establish whether an argument is sound.

DEDUCTIVE LOGIC

Validity tester

For classical propositional logic, an argument is valid if there is no truth-value assignment making every premise true and its conclusion false. The checker supports P, Q, R, NOT (¬ or !), AND (∧ or &), OR (∨ or |), and IMPLIES (→ or ->), with parentheses. It does not assess whether real-world premises are true.

PHILOSOPHY OF SCIENCE · V7

How does science produce knowledge?

Investigate scientific inference, testability, causation, induction, theory change, and the limits of scientific methods. Interactive examples are simplified models, not substitutes for real experiments.

FALSIFIABILITY

What would count against your theory?

Classify hypotheses by whether they expose themselves to a possible conflicting observation. Falsifiability is an influential criterion associated with Karl Popper, but it is not a complete or universally accepted definition of science.

INDUCTION & UNCERTAINTY

Learning from repeated observations

Simulate repeated binary observations under an assumed fixed event probability. A Beta(1,1) prior updates to Beta(1 + successes, 1 + failures). The model assumes independent, identically distributed observations.

CAUSATION VS CORRELATION

Confounding experiment

Simulate an observational comparison where a background factor affects both treatment selection and outcomes. Compare it with randomized assignment. The toy simulation uses synthetic data and does not represent any real intervention.
